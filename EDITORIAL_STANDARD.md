# Research-quality editorial standard — My Daily Feed
Effective for editions published from 16 October 2026. Existing archive remains available.

## Purpose
Publish five **independent** reports daily: AI and technology, philosophy and psychology, economics and public policy, sustainable business, and a rotating field. Each report must give readers a full causal understanding, not just an overview.

## Research first
1. State one precise central question and define key terms.
2. Consult primary papers, official datasets and major assessments before drafting. Prefer original sources over summaries.
3. Create an evidence register for material claims: claim, source URL, publisher, publication date, observation period, evidence type, uncertainty and conflict.
4. Separate measured history, estimates, forecasts, author interpretation, and *illustrative* examples. Never present illustrative values as real-world observations.
5. Seek substantive competing explanations. Weight each view by evidence. Do not invent opposition to create balance.
6. Identify potential confounding factors and cases where observed correlation does not imply causation.
7. Explain source weaknesses, representativeness and measurement limits. Mark unavailable data rather than fabricate it.

## Explanation standard
- Teach in this order where useful: problem → definitions → context → mechanism, step by step → evidence and data → worked example → competing views → real-world effects → limits → synthesis.
- Show the intermediate steps between cause and outcome. Do not leave critical transitions implied.
- At least one complete quantitative or systematic worked example; show assumptions, calculations or reasoning, interpretation and sensitivity.
- Separate facts from inferred conclusions. Each research claim in the article must link to a suitable source nearby; provide complete sources at the end.
- Keep a report self-contained. Prior editions are optional further reading only.
- Writing follows ASD-STE100 roughly 80%; clear short words with natural tone. Do not cut intellectual depth to simplify the language.
- Target 2,500–4,000 words when the subject supports it. Pass based on completeness, not word count alone.

## Diagram standard
- Decide what the visual must teach *before* producing it. Use a causal map for feedback, a schematic for physical structure, an accurate measured chart for quantities, a timeline for sequence.
- All charts must state units, source, measurement dates and whether data are real or illustrative.
- Mark no numeric scale if values are not measured. Do not treat word chains in tinted rectangles as adequate diagrams.
- Supply text interpretation and accessible alternative descriptions. Check legibility at 320 px wide and do not use color as the only encoding.
- Use shared warm earth-toned reader styles (assets/study.css and assets/reader.css). Keep visual information in HTML/SVG, not rasterized paragraphs.

## Report shape
Title and question; ~150–200-word executive summary; background; mechanism; deep analysis; meaningful visuals; real examples; opposing views; implications; 5–10 key lessons; five application-focused Q&As; dated source links. Headings can vary if this structure is preserved.

## Prepublish review
Fact check sources; inspect calculations; flag uncertainty; evaluate alternative explanations; check complete causal steps and active visual teaching; verify links and date; validate published HTML, mobile, theme and accessibility; confirm 5 topical articles in the date folder. Do not state that GitHub Pages is live until deployment is confirmed. Never generate report text only to meet a count.
